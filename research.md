## Overview

Our group studies the properties of **strongly interacting matter** under extreme conditions — in particular the **Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP)**, a state of deconfined quarks and gluons that exists at temperatures above ~150 MeV. We probe this state using ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions at the LHC and RHIC.

---

## Research Themes

### Relativistic Hydrodynamics

We develop and apply viscous relativistic fluid dynamics to describe the bulk evolution of the QGP. This includes causality constraints, transport coefficients, and comparisons with experiment.

### Initial Conditions and Pre-Equilibrium Dynamics

The geometry and fluctuations of the nuclear overlap region at the moment of collision seed all subsequent dynamics. We study how pre-hydrodynamic evolution transforms these initial conditions into the observable anisotropies.

### Flow and Collective Behavior

Collective flow — in particular anisotropic flow harmonics and momentum-dependent flow fluctuations — provides direct evidence for QGP formation and carries information about its transport properties. We develop new observables and perform quantitative comparisons with data.

### Scaled Transverse-Momentum Spectra

We identified that the shape of transverse-momentum spectra displays remarkable universality across collision systems and centralities. This provides a new lens on thermalization and the onset of collectivity, even in small systems.

### Nuclear Structure

We investigate how the shape and substructure of colliding nuclei (including deformed and light-ion systems) imprint themselves on final-state observables.

### Simulation Frameworks and Bayesian Inference

We work with full hybrid simulation chains (IP-Glasma → MUSIC → UrQMD) and use Bayesian inference, Gaussian process emulation, and machine learning to extract QGP properties from data.
