Research focus

Probing QCD matter in relativistic nuclear collisions

Led by Prof. Matthew Luzum in the Department of Mathematical Physics at IFUSP, our group combines theoretical physics and large-scale hybrid simulations to study hot and dense QCD matter.

  1. 01

    Relativistic viscous hydrodynamics

    (3+1)D viscous fluid dynamics of the Quark-Gluon Plasma, causality constraints, transport coefficients, and non-equilibrium corrections at freeze-out.

  2. 02

    Initial conditions & pre-equilibrium

    Fluctuating nuclear overlap geometry and pre-hydrodynamic kinetic evolution bridging initial energy deposition with hydrodynamic expansion.

  3. 03

    Scaled pT spectra & collectivity

    Universal scaling of transverse-momentum spectra shapes across collision systems and centralities as a precision probe of collectivity in large and small systems.

  4. 04

    Flow harmonics & correlations

    Anisotropic flow (vn), momentum-dependent flow fluctuations, principal component analysis, and collective signatures at RHIC and the LHC.

  5. 05

    Nuclear structure across energy scales

    Imprints of nuclear deformation, short-range two-nucleon correlations, and light-ion nuclear structure on high-energy heavy-ion observables.

  6. →

    Bayesian inference & software

    Multi-stage simulation chains (TRENTo, KoMPoST, MUSIC, iSS, UrQMD), Gaussian process emulators, and open-source tools.

Connected research

International collaborations

Our local research group at USP plays a leading role in multi-institution theoretical and phenomenological collaborations.

Coordination · Prof. Matthew Luzum

The ExTrEMe Collaboration

Experiment and Theory in Extreme Matter is a multi-institution network connecting researchers across Brazilian and international universities on heavy-ion collision phenomenology and hybrid hydrodynamic modeling.

SIMS Working Group Convener

JETSCAPE Collaboration

The Jet Energy-loss Tomography with a Statistically and Computationally Advanced Program Envelope collaboration develops a modular, multi-stage event generator and Bayesian inference framework for heavy-ion collisions.
